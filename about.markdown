My name is Rogelio Montemayor. I am a Senior Data Analyst at [DataCamp][datacamp], working across analytics engineering, internal tools, and AI-assisted workflows.

I was born and raised in Mexico. I studied civil engineering at Monterrey Tech (ITESM) in Mexico, then worked for BCG, a management consulting company, and then earned an MBA at the Stanford Graduate School of Business as a Fulbright Scholar. I went on to found and lead companies in real estate, mining, and energy.

During the MBA, I remember talking to the younger brother of a college friend who was studying computer science at Stanford. I told him that I thought what he was doing was very exciting and might give it a try. He said: "you are too old." I believed him and thought maybe in another life.

Twenty years later, I found myself rethinking that belief.

In 2020, I decided to take advantage of the changes brought on by the Covid-19 pandemic, plus some personal opportunities, and restart my career with a deeper focus on data and analytics. I wanted to move closer to technical work: learning how things work and building useful systems. Analytics gave me a way to combine that curiosity with the business experience I already had.

In November 2021, I joined DataCamp on a short-term contract, helping create data science competitions for learners. That contract kept getting extended, from three months to six, then a year, until I became a permanent employee. Along the way, I moved into the Analytics team.

Today, I build data models, write SQL, and develop dashboards and reporting systems that help teams understand customer behavior, product usage, and business performance. Much of my work is in B2B commercial analytics and analytics engineering, using tools such as dbt, BigQuery, Looker, and Atlan.

Increasingly, I also build internal tools and workflows where people and AI agents work together. That includes giving agents the context to find and use trusted data, helping identify and scope technical debt, and building content workflows with human review. I have been working with Claude Managed Agents and learning how much the usefulness of these systems depends on clear definitions, thoughtful integrations, and knowing where a person needs to stay involved.

My earlier career still helps me every day. Having worked on the business side, I understand how much the question behind an analysis matters, and how important it is to make the result useful to the person making a decision.

I have always loved learning, computers, and solving thought problems. I am at a stage where I want to focus more on what I want to do, what I enjoy doing, than on a title or a salary. This blog is where I share the strides and stumbles along the way, from my first programming courses to the work I am doing now. There is still so much to learn.

You can find more about my professional background on [LinkedIn][linkedin].
